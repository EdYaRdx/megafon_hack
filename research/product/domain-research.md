# Product and communication domain research

**Scope.** Product methods from actual independent ProductResearch and CommunicationResearch scout sessions plus Lead-opened primary/official pages. This is not a product-specific MegaFon strategy: no target product, market, customer sample, internal metrics or economics are established by the supplied case brief. ProductResearch output is available at `agent://ProductResearch`; CommunicationResearch output at `agent://CommunicationResearch`. Their research was read-only and used public sources. Agent conclusions remain their authors' findings; Lead synthesis below is not independent validation of their sources.

## Product strategy: decision chain, not framework decoration

Recommended chain: decision and deadline → customer, circumstances and desired progress → current workaround/status quo and substitutable alternatives → customer/business outcome → evidence-ranked assumptions → distinct options (including continue/status quo and stop where plausible) → tests that can change decision → investment/change/stop recommendation. **[INFERENCE]** This joins the local contest requirements (R02, R05–R08) with the inspected evidence below; it is not evidence about any actual MegaFon product.

### Evidence and methods

- **Jobs and context.** The Christensen Institute's JTBD page defines jobs through circumstances and forces that shape choices and supplies case examples of unexpected alternatives. Source: [Christensen Institute, “Jobs to Be Done Theory”](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/), modified 2025-05-30, sections “Definition,” case examples and “Three critical must-knows”; and [“The Forces of Progress”](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/graphic/the-forces-of-progress/), modified 2024-09-04. **DOCUMENTED.** Use as a framing lens: ask about the concrete situation, desired progress, current workaround, forces to switch/stay. Do not treat a retrospective job narrative as the exclusive or causal explanation; the cited examples are selected cases, not generalizable estimates. **INFERENCE / LIMITATION.**
- **Problem before solution.** The official [GOV.UK Service Manual “User research in discovery”](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/user-research-in-discovery), published 2016-11-18, says discovery investigates likely users, what they are trying to do, current methods/channels, problems and needs before planning/design/build; it recommends broad user range and observing, interviewing, existing data and previous research. **DOCUMENTED.** It is government-service guidance, not comparative evidence for commercial product outcomes. **LIMITATION.** Use discovery to separate requested feature from evidenced opportunity.
- **Opportunity-to-solution.** Product Talk's [Opportunity Solution Trees guide](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/), published 2023-12-06 and updated 2026-09-29, describes linking desired outcomes to needs/pain points, candidate solutions and risky-assumption tests; it recommends story-based interviews and gives 3–4 interviews as an initial heuristic. **DOCUMENTED as practitioner framework; not empirical proof.** Interviews uncover possible needs/contexts, not prevalence, willingness to pay or causal lift; the numerical heuristic is not statistical sufficiency. **LIMITATION.** Preserve multiple needs and solutions rather than hard-code one tree as truth.
- **Alternatives and competition.** The [DOJ/FTC 2023 Merger Guidelines](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2023_merger_guidelines_final_12.18.2023.pdf), issued 2023-12-18, pp. 2–3, 8–9 and §§4.2–4.3, describe fact-specific market analysis and customer substitution/competitive evidence. **DOCUMENTED.** This is U.S. merger-enforcement guidance, not a consumer product competitor template. **LIMITATION.** Use switching/substitution as questions; do not equate a broad “same job” list with close substitutes or legal market definition.
- **Retention metric validity.** [Amplitude retention calculation docs](https://www.amplitude.com/docs/analytics/charts/retention-analysis/retention-analysis-calculation), last updated 2026-11-03, distinguish exact-day “Return On” from “Return On or After”; incomplete later intervals change denominators and can make apparent aggregate curves rise. **DOCUMENTED** for Amplitude's chart. A measured decline is descriptive: inspect event definition, eligible population, cohort age/maturity, acquisition mix, instrumentation, seasonality, time zone and meaningful return event before assigning a cause. **INFERENCE.** Vendor-specific calculation behavior must not be assumed for an arbitrary dataset.
- **Economics and assumptions.** [Strategyzer's critical-hypothesis guide](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-to-test-your-idea-start-with-the-most-critical-hypotheses), Alex Osterwalder (2017-05-02), treats desirability, feasibility and viability as hypotheses to test; [Stripe CLV guide](https://stripe.com/resources/more/customer-lifetime-value), sections on CLV and its drivers (publication date not shown in opened excerpt), describes CLV as a forecast affected by margin, churn/lifespan and acquisition cost. **DOCUMENTED.** Use contribution/margin-aware, cohort-sensitive scenarios, state inputs, ranges and missing values, and compare marginal cost/foregone alternatives. CLV is forecast-sensitive and Stripe is a commercial vendor; no benchmark or MegaFon economics inferred. **LIMITATION / INFERENCE.**
- **Experiment design.** [Microsoft Research, “Patterns of Trustworthy Experimentation: During-Experiment Stage”](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-during-experiment-stage/), published 2021-01-25, recommends a holistic metric set (data-quality, overall outcome, feature diagnostics, guardrails), frequent checks, caution for repeated peeking, SRM diagnostics and stable segments; its example illustrates a novelty effect. **DOCUMENTED** as Microsoft's practice and example, not a universal rule. Pre-specify outcome, comparator, population, time window, guardrails, data-quality checks and stop/decision rule; use appropriate statistical design and don't call correlations causal. **INFERENCE.**
- **Stop/continue.** Strategyzer and Product Talk enumerate continuing, reshaping, pivoting or stopping as possible responses to assumption tests. **DOCUMENTED** as framework prescriptions, not quantitative stop thresholds. A stop path must account for test quality and opportunity cost; repeated discovery must not defer a decision indefinitely. **INFERENCE.**

### Product unknowns and counterexamples

No public source reviewed establishes actual MegaFon churn cause, cohort behavior, acquisition mix, cost, margin, demand, competitor choice, willingness to pay or product capability. All are **UNKNOWN** until supplied with locatable data. A positive association between onboarding behavior and retention would not establish that onboarding causes retention; an aggregate trend can reflect mix or incomplete cohorts; a beloved product may fail viability; an interview-derived “job” can be non-representative; competitor page features do not prove demand. These counterexamples constrain claims and tests.

## Communication strategy: evidence follows the promise

A communication recommendation is provisional until a product decision and current evidence exist. Every candidate promise should trace as: **audience/context → choice/understanding barrier → intended inference/message → product capability/term → evidence and qualification → channel role → test**. If current product evidence cannot support the wording's audience, geography, time, outcome or comparative scope, qualify or withhold it. **INFERENCE / RECOMMENDED.**

### Communication experiments

1. Do not treat the current catalog as the control or infer a “generic benefits” campaign. First document the actual current customer-facing communication, product/region/terms, evidenced customer job/barrier, eligible population and what decision the test will change. If no current control can be observed, define a proposed baseline explicitly rather than labeling it current. **RECOMMENDED; current baseline/barrier UNKNOWN.**
2. A first comprehension/usability test can assess correct recall of price period, package, eligibility/exclusions, task completion and confidence; it tests task comprehension, not segment prevalence or sales incrementality. **RECOMMENDED.**
3. Only after the above prerequisites, randomized holdout can estimate incremental effect for that design/population/window. Predefine one changed factor, primary outcome/denominator/attribution window, guardrails, baseline variance and powered sample/duration, exclusions, consent/privacy/ad compliance, contamination and stop rules. No performance data or forecast is available. **RECOMMENDED / INFERENCE.**
4. Channel roles (product page for detail, awareness creative, support/FAQ) remain functional hypotheses, not a performance-backed plan. Map proposed execution to channel-specific requirements. Current consolidated 38-FZ [Art. 18](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_58968/f892dec1383709792452f18d36e7043306e2be0a/) part 1 documents prior consent for telecom-network advertising; [Art. 18.1](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_58968/2c4537e4796f6ff8b2736ed1b0d4fef08e14458e/) parts 16–17 document labeling/advertiser/ad-identifier rules for covered internet ads. Applicability/classification and other requirements require current legal review; no campaign clearance is claimed.

## Evidence integrity and revision rule

Separate direct observations, source statements, interpretation, and future test. Record source version/date/region and the denominator/time window for data claims. A new data point or changed page does not erase the old record: mark affected claims, options and decisions stale; preserve previous wording/decision; re-evaluate; change recommendation only with rationale and approver. This is a recommended state transition, not tested product behavior.
