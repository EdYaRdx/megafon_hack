# Options and trade-offs

All values refer to the fictional scenario; no option has execution approval. The R2 capacity and deadline statements are claimed, not independently verified.

## Option A — status quo / freeze

- What changes: keep the current flow; do not build, recruit, deploy, promote, or make a retention claim.
- Value and cost: avoids immediate execution risk and respects the claimed cap; leaves the complaint unanswered. Quantified value/cost unknown.
- Evidence / assumptions: week-four return contrast is descriptive only; resource restriction and dates are claimed.
- Strongest objection: a real setup problem may persist while no learning occurs.
- Falsifier / revisit: verified capacity, owner approval, and a defined diagnostic scope become available.

## Option B — decision readiness (recommended interim step)

- What changes: use only approved existing capacity to define the return event/window, user segment, task, success threshold, partner audience/channel/capability, and approval path; do not build or recruit.
- Value and cost: makes a later diagnostic or communication decision testable without spending the claimed scarce capacity on an unapproved prototype. Exact cost and capacity are unknown.
- Evidence / assumptions: `C-01`–`C-04` and new claimed constraints `C-06`–`C-09`; no retention win is established.
- Strongest objection: paperwork may not change week-four use and the capacity claim may be wrong.
- Falsifier / revisit: owner verifies a different capacity/date premise or supplies an approved, feasible alternative.

## Option C — simplify setup diagnostic

- What changes: after 2027-01-15 only if the claimed restriction is confirmed to expire, run the approved low-fidelity paired task diagnostic before any shorter-setup build.
- Value and cost: may identify a first-week blocker; cost unknown. Ops estimate of two engineer-weeks is unverified and conflicts with the claimed interim cap.
- Evidence / assumptions: setup completion 30/48 current vs 26/48 checklist; cause not identified. Hypothesis `C-05`.
- Strongest objection: diagnostic could consume time without changing week-four return; sample cannot estimate prevalence/effect.
- Falsifier / revisit: no repeated actionable setup blocker, or critical task/accessibility errors.

## Option D — checklist polish

- What changes: defer; do not promote or build the checklist while the contrast is confounded and capacity is constrained.
- Value and cost: 31/48 eligible checklist accounts opened it; no value proven. Ops estimate of one engineer-week is unverified.
- Evidence / assumptions: `C-01`/`C-02`; checklist arm has 22/48 returns vs 18/48, but assignment/definitions unknown and support count higher.
- Strongest objection: deferral may miss a useful intervention.
- Falsifier / revisit: a later controlled test or comprehension evidence meets a predeclared threshold.

## Option E — partner promotion

- What changes: do not send the requested copy; first obtain the partner list, audience, channel, approved capability, substantiated claim, and written approval.
- Value and cost: reach/ROI unknown; audience/channel/authority unknown.
- Evidence / assumptions: `C-08`/`C-09`; the remembered R1 sign-off is not a record and cannot be reused.
- Strongest objection: a deadline may be missed.
- Falsifier / revisit: complete partner brief, validated capability/evidence, and matching approval for the exact R2 message and scope.

## Option F — stop / close

- What changes: close the checklist/retention initiative and spend no further effort.
- Value and cost: removes opportunity cost; possible lost benefit unknown.
- Evidence / assumptions: evidence quality is weak; no evidence product has no value.
- Strongest objection: abandons a potentially solvable issue before a bounded test.
- Falsifier / revisit: owner identifies a user-validated task problem and approves learning budget.
