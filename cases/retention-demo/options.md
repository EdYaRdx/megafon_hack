# Options — synthetic case

All figures and scenarios below refer only to the fictional fixture. Costs, real customer impact, and company economics are `UNKNOWN`.

## A — Keep the current product; verify measurement and cohort mix first (recommended next step)

- **Mechanism:** reconcile the event contract and extraction; compare mature, fixed-window cohorts by stable acquisition channel; check period completeness, campaign/release changes, and data quality before changing product or spend.
- **Evidence:** synthetic cohort rates remain constant within channel while mix changes (`E-001..E-005`). Method sources document comparable retention intervals and data-quality checks (`E-005..E-006`).
- **Assumptions:** a real owner can provide correctly scoped data; this synthetic channel pattern is not presumed to describe reality.
- **Benefits:** reduces risk of an intervention aimed at an aggregate-composition artifact.
- **Costs / risks:** delays intervention if a genuine within-segment decline is harming users; analyst time is `UNKNOWN`.
- **Strong objection:** diagnosis alone does not help users if a real service problem is already harming them.
- **Falsifier / next test:** validated, mature real cohorts show a material within-segment decline under a stable event definition.

## B — Run a targeted onboarding intervention for a diagnosed segment

- **Mechanism:** after discovery identifies a specific onboarding friction, test a reversible change against a concurrent control among eligible users in that segment.
- **Evidence:** none in this case establishes an onboarding problem. This is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- **Assumptions:** a specific friction is evidenced; intervention is reversible; approvals and measurement are available.
- **Benefits:** could address a real user problem with a bounded intervention.
- **Costs / risks:** engineering effort and user harm are `UNKNOWN`; risks include moving the wrong proxy or masking an acquisition-quality issue.
- **Strong objection:** the proposed mechanism is currently invented; launching now turns an aggregate pattern into a causal story.
- **Falsifier / next test:** discovery does not identify the friction, or a pre-registered test fails the owner-approved threshold/guardrails.

## C — Pause or reduce the lowest-retention acquisition channel

- **Mechanism:** change channel spend or eligibility rather than product experience.
- **Evidence:** the fixture's paid segment has lower synthetic D30 than organic, but no cost, lifetime value, incremental conversion, or user-mix evidence is supplied (`E-003`).
- **Assumptions:** lower D30 means worse economics or user outcomes; attribution is valid; the pause is reversible.
- **Benefits:** may reduce low-quality acquisition cost if unit economics confirm the concern.
- **Costs / risks:** lost reach/revenue and partner commitments are `UNKNOWN`.
- **Strong objection:** retention alone is not channel profitability; a pause based on this table could destroy value.
- **Falsifier / next test:** attributed cohort contribution margin shows the channel is viable, or the gap disappears after comparable segmentation.

## D — Stop the direction

- **Mechanism:** discontinue the hypothetical product or retention work.
- **Evidence:** none on user need, strategic fit, costs, or viability.
- **Assumptions:** the product is not viable or strategically relevant; unsupported here.
- **Benefits:** avoids investment if an independently verified stop condition is met.
- **Costs / risks:** opportunity cost and migration/support impact are `UNKNOWN`.
- **Strong objection:** there is no evidence sufficient to justify closure.
- **Falsifier / next test:** verified user value and viable economics would oppose closure.

## Comparison

A is the only defensible immediate next step in this fixture because it improves the measurement basis without asserting a cause. This is a recommendation to investigate, not an approved organizational decision. B, C, and D remain conditional; none is authorized by this case.
