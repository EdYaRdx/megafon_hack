# Brief — synthetic service-launch exercise

**Scenario status:** entirely fictional. No company, users, interviews, or operations are represented.

## Starting request

> “Should we launch our meal-planning add-on next month? The beta survey looks encouraging.”

## Decision to support

Whether to commit to a paid public launch of a fictional meal-planning add-on, first run bounded problem discovery/prototype validation, keep the current product unchanged, or stop the direction.

## Intake record

- **Decision owner:** UNKNOWN; hypothetical product lead in this exercise.
- **Audience / user job:** UNKNOWN. No actual user research or validated segment.
- **Desired outcome / deadline:** “next month” is a request, not an approved date or success condition.
- **Constraints:** public sources and explicitly synthetic materials only; no actual user contact, external launch, spend, or company-data use authorized.
- **Materials:** `input/beta-survey.csv` (invented counts); `input/concept-note.md` (fictional one-page concept); one directly opened public method page, recorded in `sources.jsonl`.
- **Missing:** respondent recruitment and question wording; product capability and delivery cost; price/economics; actual user need; launch approval; success threshold; service/support and accessibility requirements.

## Research questions tied to decisions

1. What does the survey actually establish about the intended population? If sampling is not representative, do not use it to forecast demand or launch conversion.
2. What user problem and current workaround does the add-on address? If unknown, choose discovery before paid launch.
3. Can the proposed capability and unit economics support a paid promise? If not, do not write a price or outcome claim.
4. What evidence and owner-approved criteria would justify a limited prototype, launch, or stop?
