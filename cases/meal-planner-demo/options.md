# Options — synthetic service launch

All users, counts, concepts, costs, and outcomes are fictional. No option is approved.

## A — Keep the current product unchanged; launch nothing now (status quo)

- **Action:** leave the existing hypothetical service as-is and make no meal-planning promise.
- **Value/cost:** avoids unsupported commitments; opportunity cost and team capacity are UNKNOWN.
- **Basis:** feature not built, user/job and economics UNKNOWN (`C-003`, `C-005`).
- **Strongest objection:** preserves uncertainty and could miss a meaningful user need or timely opportunity.
- **Revisit/falsifier:** credible evidence identifies a material user problem and a feasible, safe capability.

## B — Run bounded problem discovery, then an opt-in prototype only if a real need is evidenced (recommended next step for this exercise)

- **Action:** first study real user goals/workarounds with consent and appropriate recruitment; prototype a narrow idea only after evidence identifies a job and safety constraints. Human approval required before contact.
- **Value/cost:** improves decision quality while delaying paid launch; research and prototype costs, recruitment, effect, and timeline are UNKNOWN.
- **Basis:** initial survey evidence is non-random (`C-001`, `C-002`); revision-2 fictional exercise has both positive reports and counterexamples but is not real evidence (`C-006`); discovery method source supports investigating current goals/problems (`C-004`).
- **Strongest objection:** discovery costs time and may be unnecessary if competitors or existing behavior already establish the need; no such evidence was supplied here.
- **Revisit/falsifier:** discovery finds no recurring, consequential unmet need, or a safe/feasible prototype cannot be built within owner-approved constraints.

## C — Launch a paid service next month based on the survey

- **Action:** commit to build, price, and promote a service on the requested date.
- **Value/cost:** could capture an opportunity sooner; build cost, revenue, demand, safety, support, and date feasibility are all UNKNOWN.
- **Basis:** only 15 synthetic clicks from a non-random tester cohort (`C-001`, `C-002`); a second synthetic note does not validate demand (`C-006`).
- **Strongest objection:** the central user need, product capability, safe dietary handling, economics, and conversion are unvalidated; this risks both a false promise and wasted delivery.
- **Revisit/falsifier:** verified user need, capability/safety review, credible price/economic case, operational readiness, and an owner-approved launch gate.

## D — Stop the meal-planning direction

- **Action:** close exploration and allocate no further work.
- **Value/cost:** avoids future investment; opportunity cost is UNKNOWN.
- **Basis:** no evidence of demand or viability is not itself evidence of no demand (`C-005`).
- **Strongest objection:** premature closure may discard a valuable need before it has been investigated.
- **Revisit/falsifier:** independently gathered evidence of a consequential, underserved user job and a feasible, viable response.

## Recommendation

Option B is a **recommendation for human review**, not an organizational decision. The available evidence rules out treating the synthetic survey as launch validation; it does not prove discovery will find demand. Defer paid launch until a real user problem, safe scope, delivery economics, and approval criteria are evidenced. Keep A and D as legitimate alternatives if the owner declines research or discovery fails.
